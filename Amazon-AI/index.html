Find shared language to co-create.

Find shared language to co-create.

I spent a lot of time working with data scientists to build AI models. when building worlds, we often explored similar experiments and assumptions.

They use technical intuition and I study behavior and systems. We communicate through diagrams of observations, entities, outcomes, and systems.

For every collaborator, there is a shared language; find it and find a key.

What did I learn?

Share thoughtfully.

Share thoughtfully.

This work caused such excitement and potential, it was closely observed by executive leadership at Amazon. I was responsible for owning monthly progress review with these leaders. Each review was only 25 minutes long.


I relied on the following format to effectively lead each review:

What we want

We want to achieve this business outcome

How we think we’ll get it

If we seed this behavior (prototype) we will achieve the outcome

Why we think that

We can achieve the outcome through that behavior because of this evidence

When it should happen

Here is when we’ll deliver to users

How we’ll know we did it

Here is how we’ll know we’ve successfully reached that outcome

Most problems are search problems.

Most problems are search problems.

When we help users get to what they need, it’s through search. Navigating, way-finding, browsing, personalizing, recommending are all search problems.

Search doesn’t have to be the action of a human. An intelligent experience should search on behalf of them; surfacing anticipatory suggestions that appropriately drive behavior.

Articulation is a barrier.

Articulation is a barrier.

Humans simply struggle to articulate their way into getting the results they desire through prompting. It requires back and forth and long streams of text,
which inevitably impacts usability.

Instead, identify their motive through data and anticipate it in the experience.


Develop a bit of of contrarian thinking.

Develop a bit of of contrarian thinking.

Working with emerging and revolutionary technology can actualize powerful experiences, but it also means you can have dramatic impacts on people, so you must embrace the responsibility of defending their rights.

AI is not a feature you need to market.

AI is not a feature you need to market.

In 2021, the trend of labeling every feature with "AI" was already on the rise, but I led us down a different path. I maintained our focus on the quality of the user experience rather than the underlying technology.

After all, the goal is to provide the best possible service, regardless of whether it involves machine learning, natural language processing, or any other form of AI.
